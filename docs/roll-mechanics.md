# Roll Mechanics — Concept Slate

Fourth in series. Spins, crates, luck multipliers, rarity ladders. 15 August 2026.

Supersedes `chance-mechanics.md` as the relevant slate — that doc chased press-your-luck
staking, which is a different genre from the standard Roblox RNG kit covered here.

---

## 1. The finding: the roll should be the engine, not the game

Three data points pointing one direction.

**Pure rollers get enormous traffic and monetize gently.** Sol's RNG has **2.08 billion
visits** and holds 40–100K concurrent. Its Quick Roll pass — which only skips the reveal
animation — is 100 R$, and **Auto Roll is free** via group join. Coverage describes the
pricing as transparent with nothing feeling mandatory. That restraint is deliberate and it's
why players love it.

**The classic loot-box format is fading.** Pet Simulator 99 has 2.4B visits but has fallen
hard from its 2024 peak; Pet Simulator X is down to roughly a thousand concurrent.
Incremental simulators show real audience fatigue. Don't build a pet sim.

**What's ascending is the roll attached to a system.** Tycoons had declined since 2024 and
are now in renaissance — Grow a Garden 2 at #1, Steal a Brainrot Tycoon at #6. Build a Base
RNG's whole premise is that the roll feeds a base.

Rolling is a fantastic *engine* and a thin *game*. The question that matters isn't how the
roll looks. It's **what job the rolled thing does afterward.**

### The tradeoff being chosen

Maximum monetization was the brief. Sol's RNG proves the top of this genre deliberately
leaves money on the table — free auto-roll, cheap convenience passes, nothing mandatory —
and buys retention, rating and word-of-mouth with it. You *can* monetize a roller much
harder. You trade rating for ARPPU when you do, and rating is distribution on this platform.
Build to Defend Loot: 88% rating, 10.6-min sessions. Build a Base RNG: 99%.
**Pick the point on that curve deliberately.**

## 2. Craft: making a roll feel good

Six mechanisms, all cheap, mostly overlooked. The difference between a roller people grind
for months and one they bounce off in a day.

| Mechanism | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The wait before the reveal *is* the product. A roll that resolves instantly has no tension — which is exactly why Sol's can charge 100 R$ to *remove* the animation. Build the buildup, then sell the skip |
| **Near-miss** | Show what you almost got. A reveal that passes through higher tiers before settling, or a counter stalling one tick short, beats a flat result by a mile. Highest-leverage animation in the game |
| **Broadcast** | Announce rare pulls server-wide. Costs nothing; turns other people's luck into your motivation. Free virality |
| **Pity** | Guarantee something at a known count. A hard pity at ~200 rolls converts despair into a countdown. Players who see the floor keep rolling |
| **Conditional rarity** | Gate some rarities behind conditions, not just odds — weather, time of day, equipped items, failure streaks. Monarch's trick. Converts a number game into a discovery game with a wiki community |
| **Session luck** | Let luck climb the longer you stay, reset daily. One dial rewarding both long sessions and daily returns |

rolling — but carries unknowns Payroll doesn't.

## 6. Gauntlet — Stage 2: red team

**Against Payroll.** It's an idle game wearing an RNG costume, and idle games are quiet.
Virality scores lowest for a reason: watching a number rise produces nothing worth clipping.
Sol's gets shared because a one-in-three-billion pull is an *event*; a worker earning 4% more
is not.
*Mitigation:* make the top of the ladder absurd rather than incremental. A legendary worker
shouldn't earn 40% more — it should earn 40× more, arrive with a server-wide announcement,
and visibly change the base. Compress the middle of the curve so the top reads as an event.

**Against Three Reels.** Combination rolling is untested on Roblox and the reason may not be
that nobody thought of it. Combinations demand a mental model of what pairs with what — real
cognitive load on an audience that decides in under thirty seconds. If the logic isn't
legible at a glance it reads as noise.
*Mitigation:* prototype the reveal before anything else. If a ten-year-old can't tell within
two seconds whether a result was good, the concept is dead — worth knowing in week one
rather than week nine.

## 7. Verdict

**Payroll as the frame. Overgrow's mutation math as the rarity engine. The Almanac's
conditional rarities as the endgame.**

Payroll gives every pull a permanent consequence you can watch accrue, which is what pure
rollers lack. Overgrow's multiplicative mutations sit on top of the workers so value
compounds instead of adding — that's where uncapped whale spend lives. The Almanac's
condition-gated rarities are the hour-40 answer, keeping a wiki community alive long after
the base ladder is exhausted.

### Build order

1. **Weeks 1–2 · The reveal.** One roll button, one rarity ladder, one great reveal animation
   with near-miss built in. No workers, no economy. If the pull isn't satisfying with nothing
   attached, nothing downstream saves it.
2. **Weeks 3–4 · The job.** Workers, posts, offline accrual. Every pull now moves a number
   that keeps moving after you close the game. Ship 2× Luck and the first potion.
3. **Weeks 5–6 · The compounding.** Mutations on workers, multiplicative. Server-wide
   broadcast on rare pulls. Hard pity at a published count.
4. **Weeks 7–8 · The hunt.** Conditional rarities, index, completion buffs. This converts
   week-two players into month-three players.
5. **Month 3+ · Seasons.** Rotating pools and limited ladders — scarcity clock and season-pass
   hook. Sol's calls these eras; it's the right pattern.

### What has to be true

- **The rarity curve must be compressed in the middle and absurd at the top.** A legendary
  earning 40% more is a stat. One earning 40× is a story, and stories are how this genre grows.
- **Pity must be published, not hidden.** A visible floor turns a losing streak into a
  countdown. Does more for retention than any luck multiplier you sell.
- **Every pull must move something permanent.** If a bad pull leaves the player exactly where
  they started, you've built a slot machine with worse odds than a slot machine. Even a
  common should nudge accrual up.

### One build note

If you sell anything that *is* a random pull, Roblox wants the odds shown, so you'll need an
odds screen in the UI. Selling luck multipliers and potions instead of selling pulls sidesteps
it entirely and converts better anyway — the structure Sol's and Build a Base RNG both use.
