# Cross-Genre Slate — The Copycat Premium

Fifth in series. 15 August 2026.

The previous four slates all converged on the same corner: roll something, it earns offline,
defend it. This one goes wide into the genres that convergence skipped.

---

## 1. A correction: novelty was over-weighted

Across the earlier slates I weighted *defensibility* at 10% and used it to kill concepts — a
pure-aura idea died for competing with Sol's, a garrison concept scored 3/10 for being
derivative. The reasoning was that an occupied slot is a losing fight.

**Fish It says otherwise.**

| Month (2025) | Avg CCU |
|---|---|
| August | 50,000 |
| September | 186,000 |
| October | 389,000 |
| November | 839,000 |
| December | 1,000,000+ |
| Peak since | **2,700,000** |

Fish It introduced no new idea. It took Fisch's loop, stripped it down, made the flow from
one action to the next nearly immediate, added a built-in AFK mode — and outgrew the original
roughly tenfold. Coverage is blunt that it succeeded **not because it was original but
because it was accessible.**

> The moat isn't novelty. It's being the version a new player can actually get into — and the
> incumbent's complexity is the opening, not the barrier.

Corollary: Fisch didn't lose to a better game. It lost partly on **monetization concerns**.

## 2. The uncomfortable finding: lighter monetization is earning more

Three independent cases this month, all pointing the same way.

| Game | Scale | Posture | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sailor Piece** | 420K avg CCU, 743.7K peak | Markets itself as "fully playable for free"; community agrees. Explicitly positioned against Blox Fruits' monetization depth | **Est. $2.13M/month** — more than Steal a Brainrot's ~$1.4M, on a game nine months old |
| **Sol's RNG** | 2.08B visits, 40–100K CCU | Auto-roll free via group join. Headline pass is 100 R$ to skip an animation. Nothing feels mandatory | Two years atop its genre, still among the platform's top earners |
| **Fisch → Fish It** | 4.6B visits → 2.7M peak CCU | Fisch's monetization drew complaints; Fish It shipped the gentler version | The lighter clone overtook the original |

**Maximum revenue and maximum monetization pressure are different targets, and on Roblox
they have been diverging.** Rating is distribution here — the algorithm and word-of-mouth both
key off it, and a 99% game gets shown to people an 88% game doesn't. Price gently, keep
everything genuinely optional, take margin from volume. Currently the higher-earning position,
not a compromise.

## 3. Five mechanics the earlier slates missed

- **Cooperative asymmetry.** Vet Clinic ships ten roles (Intern, Surgeon, Security, Head
  Nurse), each changing what you can do — and your friends need you to be a *different* class
  than they are. Converts playing together from preference into requirement. Peak 736,948
  concurrent, 95% rating.
- **Trading as the second game.** MM2 is twelve years old and just peaked at 1.1M concurrent
  with 60M+ daily visits. Seven tiers, trading gated at level 10, community value lists where
  a top knife runs ~100,000. Deepest long-term retention mechanic on the platform.
- **AFK inside the active game.** Fish It's 30-minute average session comes from a built-in
  AFK mode — not a separate idle game, an idle mode inside an active one. Cheap, and it moves
  the metric everyone under-optimizes.
- **Cozy surface, real loss underneath.** The pattern behind 2026's horror breakouts: a
  friendly, legible premise with genuine stakes beneath. Survival horror retains longer than
  experience horror because it adds progression and upgradeable gear to the tension.
- **Progression speed as the wedge.** Sailor Piece's differentiator over Blox Fruits is that
  it progresses faster. "The same thing but it respects your time" is a complete strategy. It
  also jumped 66% in a day when One Piece season two hit Netflix — external culture has a
  public release calendar.

## 7. Verdict

**The Kinder Raid — attack the disliked incumbent, not the beloved one.**

Night Shift scores marginally higher but would launch against Vet Clinic at a **95% rating**
and a 736K peak. Players love it; there's no complaint to exploit.

Build to Defend Loot sits at **88% with 6,496 downvotes and a 10.6-minute average session**,
on 81 million visits of proven demand. That's the Fish It setup precisely: a proven loop whose
leader people keep bouncing off. You aren't asking players to try a new idea — you're offering
the version of a thing they already want that doesn't annoy them.

Graft Night Shift's cooperative asymmetry on: co-op base defense with roles that need each
other supplies the social layer async games structurally lack.

### Build order

1. **Weeks 1–2 · The morning report.** Build a base, survive a PvE night, read the report.
   Defense pays out from the first build. If the report isn't a story, stop.
2. **Weeks 3–4 · Simplify ruthlessly.** This is the whole strategy — every system a new player
   must understand before their first good moment is a system to cut.
3. **Weeks 5–6 · Real bases.** Snapshots, bot-seeded pool, real player raids. Bots stay
   permanently for cold-start and bracket padding.
4. **Weeks 7–8 · Co-op roles.** Asymmetric defender classes so friends need each other. Add the
   AFK interleave here for session length.
5. **Month 3+ · Trading.** Gate behind a progression milestone, MM2-style. Never at minute one.

### What has to be true

- **You have to actually ship it simpler.** The instinct under competitive pressure is to add
  features the incumbent lacks, which inverts the entire strategy. Fish It won by having *less*.
- **Price gently and take margin from volume.** Three independent cases point the same way, and
  rating is distribution.
- **Defense must out-earn an average raid.** Carried from the earlier slate and still the
  load-bearing ratio — it's what makes the report good news.
